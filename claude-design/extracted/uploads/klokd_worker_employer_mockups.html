Worker UI — design rationale
Dark shell
Reduces battery drain on low-end Android devices. Familiar to Kenyan app users — M-Pesa, Bolt, and banking apps all use dark or ink UIs.
Earnings first
The KES amount is the largest, most prominent element on the shift detail screen. Workers need to see what they earn before anything else — every other detail is secondary.
Payment guarantee strip
The green left-border strip on shift detail is a deliberate trust signal. It addresses the single biggest worker anxiety — will I actually get paid? — before they have to ask.
One action per screen
Every screen has one primary action — Accept, Clock in, Submit. The secondary action (Decline) is visually subordinate. Cognitive load is minimised for a user making a fast decision.
Reputation always visible
Show-up rate, rating, and shift count sit on the home screen dashboard. Workers need to feel the value of their reputation data — it is their portable career asset on Klokd.
Celebration on payment
The paid screen is a moment of positive reinforcement. The large green checkmark and KES amount in electric mint create an emotional reward loop that drives repeat usage and word of mouth.

Employer UI — design rationale
Light shell, dark card
The employer app uses a light mist background — a deliberate contrast to the worker app. Two distinct identities on the same brand system signal that Klokd understands both sides of the relationship.
Dashboard status at a glance
Confirmed vs pending shifts surfaced immediately on open. A restaurant manager checking their phone at 7am needs the staffing situation in one second — not after navigation.
Rate intelligence strip
The suggested rate banner on shift posting — grounded in real platform data — positions Klokd as an advisor, not just a tool. This builds trust and improves worker acceptance rates, which is good for the employer.
Ranked worker cards
Workers sorted by proximity and rating with progressive opacity — the best match is visually dominant, alternatives recede. The employer's eye goes straight to the right choice without cognitive work.
Show-up rate is prominent
Reliability percentage sits directly under the worker name on every card. This is the employer's primary anxiety — not rating, not distance — will this person show up? The data answers it before the question is asked.
CTA consistency
Every primary action uses the same electric-mint-to-volt gradient CTA. The employer learns quickly — if it is green, it is the right move. Consistency reduces hesitation and increases conversion through the core loop.
